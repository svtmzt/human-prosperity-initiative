# Adoption and replication framework V1

**Status:** implemented as public toolkit, 16 November 2026. **No organisation has adopted, endorsed or partnered with HPI.**

## Purpose

Make reproducible methods and, eventually, independently assessed solutions easy for legitimate institutions, researchers and communities to discover, scrutinise and adapt — without HPI operating local interventions.

## Adaptation readiness matrix

Nine dimensions, each with questions and a required specialist reviewer: legal and regulatory compatibility; institutional feasibility; language and accessibility; local data availability; scientific transferability; costs and resources; ethics, privacy and safeguarding; human capacity; measurement feasibility. Status vocabulary: not assessed, not met, partially met, met with evidence, not applicable. A dimension counts as met only when evidence is linked. Fieldwork, recruitment, personal-data processing or outreach require every dimension met with evidence and specialist review.

The website self-check runs entirely in the browser — no transmission, storage, cookies or analytics — and produces a downloadable Markdown or JSON plan.

## Recording adoption and partnerships

Entries in `adoption.json` require the organisation, the solution, a public HTTPS evidence URL, a verification date and a verifier independent of HPI. Usage counts, download statistics and testimonials are not recorded. Both registers are empty.

## Contribution, challenge and correction

- Public issue templates: *correction or scientific challenge* and *context or source proposal*.
- Pull requests must pass `npm run check`.
- Corrections are appended to `corrections.json`; superseded documents stay published with a notice.
- No confidential intake exists; personal, welfare or health details must never be posted (see issue #26).

## Translation and accessibility

English is complete. The platform summary and responsible-tools guidance exist in Italian, unreviewed. Adapting institutions must provide materials in the languages people use and keep non-digital channels. Automated accessibility checks do not replace testing with assistive-technology users.

## Institutional integration

An institution adapting a package should: assess readiness; obtain local legal, ethics and methods review; register a protocol before enrolment; use lawful administrative data for verified outcomes; publish results including null findings; and, if it wishes to be listed as an adopter, publish evidence that an independent party can verify.
