# HPI theory of change — Global Prosperity Platform V0.1

**Status:** candidate strategy authored under founder authorisation to develop the revised model, 16 November 2026. **Not** independently reviewed, not ratified by any board and not an amendment to the founding charter (see [the charter addendum note](strategic-operating-model-v0.1.md#charter-implications)).

## The chain

1. **Global evidence.** Published research and official statistics are catalogued with provenance, rights and limitations.
2. **Recurring mechanisms.** Barriers that may operate in many places (for example compliance burden in benefit access) are identified, and each is labelled as *evidence-linked* or *candidate hypothesis*.
3. **Scientifically credible solution designs.** Intervention hypotheses are turned into evaluation packages: outcome definitions, comparison requirements, harms, costs and approval gates.
4. **Independent evaluation.** Accountable institutions, independent of HPI, run and review studies under ethics approval.
5. **Institutional and community adaptation.** Others adapt methods to their own rules, languages and people, using readiness checks.
6. **Verified changes in people's lives.** Durable, measured improvement, attributed credibly, with distributional effects reported.

HPI can do steps 1–3 now. Steps 4–6 depend on others and on approvals HPI does not hold.

## Four levels that must never be conflated

| Level | Meaning | How it is evidenced | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical reach | Software and pages exist and work | Tests, deployments | Achieved for the platform MVP |
| Research usefulness | Researchers can find, inspect and reuse something valuable | Independent use and critique | Plausible; not yet evidenced by any external user |
| Adoption | An accountable organisation uses a method | Public, independently verified evidence | None |
| Demonstrated human benefit | People's lives measurably improved | Approved study with credible comparison | None |

A visit, a download or an application is not a benefit.

## Assumptions

- **A1.** Some barriers recur across contexts closely enough that a shared method saves effort. *Partly supported* for benefit access (all three contexts share an eligibility → application → approval → receipt pathway); untested elsewhere.
- **A2.** Openly published methods lower the cost for qualified institutions to run better evaluations. *Untested.*
- **A3.** Institutions value transparent limits and gates more than confident claims. *Untested.*
- **A4.** Deterministic validation and provenance make AI-assisted research more trustworthy. *Design assumption*; no AI output is published yet.
- **A5.** Systems-level research can inform practical work without imposing an ideology. *Design commitment*; depends on independent scrutiny.

## Dependencies

Independent methodologists and ethics bodies; lawful data access via accountable institutions; local partners with legitimacy; funding for evaluation; a legal operating structure for HPI itself (F0-04).

## Counterfactuals

What would happen without HPI? Existing services (for example Our Newham Money, CRAS in Recife, patronati in Naples) already help people claim. Researchers already publish reviews and evidence maps (for example 3ie). HPI's additionality must therefore come from **cross-context reusable methods, transparent gaps and open tooling**, not from duplicating services. If additionality cannot be shown, the correct decision is to stop or to contribute to existing efforts.

## Necessary institutional capacity

An accountable legal entity, a data controller, an independent scientific review mechanism, safeguarding with a staffed confidential channel, and partners with authority over the services being studied. None exists yet.

## Risks and possible negative outcomes

- Encouraging applications that fail, adding burden without receipt.
- Overconfidence from a polished website being mistaken for validated knowledge.
- Diverting attention or resources from existing local services.
- Re-identification risks if local data are ever handled.
- AI-generated text being mistaken for evidence.
- A small, highly deprived population being deprioritised because it is "less scalable" (explicitly prohibited by the prioritisation method).

## Conditions for replication

The mechanism is documented locally; eligibility rules and institutions are mapped; the primary outcome is lawfully measurable; readiness dimensions are met with evidence; and an independent evaluator is involved.

## How AI changes the economics of knowledge production — and what it does not change

AI can reduce the cost of discovering, classifying and summarising sources and of drafting structured claims for review. It cannot verify a claim without a source, obtain ethics approval, build trust with communities, gain lawful data access or produce a counterfactual. In the HPI design AI output is a hypothesis queue for humans, never evidence, and the platform works fully without it.

## Measurement principles

Report reach, usefulness, adoption and benefit separately; never use reach as a surrogate for impact; publish null and negative results; report distributional effects and harms; state uncertainty and missing data explicitly.
